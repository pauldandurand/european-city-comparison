Annual sunshine: model and Aix station exception

Sunshine hours/year are annual estimates, not the number of sunny days. All 21 values are embedded in the page for reliable offline operation.

The original 20 cities use Sunshine Map (2026.11), modeled from NASA POWER climate normals (2001–2020) and sampled around airports. Aix-en-Provence uses 2,873 hours from a Météo-France station normal (1991–2020), because a corresponding Sunshine Map figure could not be verified. Aix therefore is not strictly methodologically comparable in this column. All values are rounded to whole hours.

The earlier “sunny days ≥60% daylight” measure was removed because historical daily calculations did not reliably complete for all cities; annual sunshine hours are a more reproducible indicator with complete coverage.

Housing prices: municipal market benchmarks

Rent (€/m²/month) is an indicative monthly residential rental price; Home price (€/m²) is an indicative sale-price benchmark. France uses November 2026 MeilleursAgents apartment market estimates. Spain, Portugal and Italy use Idealista residential listing indices from September 2026. Figures are not necessarily medians of identical 80 m² two-bedroom homes; property types, asking versus estimated prices, and provider methods differ.

Municipality boundaries

Population refers to the city proper (municipality). Density normally divides the 2023 municipal population by full administrative land area, including undeveloped land. Aix-en-Provence is the sole exception: its 10,400 people/km²* refers to the broader city center (26,000 residents / 2.5 km²), including the historic center and the first surrounding ring, as defined in an AUPA 2019 city-center study. The municipality-wide density would be about 805 people/km². The * Broader city center note appears beside the sorting status below the table.

Cross-country data caution

Poverty and higher-education measures are from differing national sources, age groups and years. French poverty is individual disposable-income based, Italy's comparison uses households, and Portugal's is based on declared gross income. The default poverty sort is numerical, not a harmonized European ranking.
